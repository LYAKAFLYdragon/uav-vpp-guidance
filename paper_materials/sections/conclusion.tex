\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a hierarchical guidance architecture that combines an optional virtual pursuit point (VPP) generator with a line-of-sight rate (LOS-rate) guidance law, trained and tuned via a bilevel PPO and CEM-EMA framework. Three main conclusions emerge from our study.

First, the dominant performance driver in the hierarchical architecture is guidance-law gain optimization. CEM-EMA tuning improves success rate from $60.2\%$ to $74.7\%$ ($+14.47$ percentage points), while the optional VPP offset provides no statistically significant advantage over a zero-offset design.

Second, the VPP offset layer is neither essential nor actively detrimental. A controlled 5-seed comparison finds comparable success rates for VPP-enabled and zero-offset policies ($70.0\%$ vs $73.3\%$, McNemar exact $p = 0.063$). The earlier report of VPP collapse to $0\%$ was an artifact of a fixed domain-randomization seed, not a property of the architecture.

Third, the hierarchical design is more stable and sample-efficient than end-to-end DRL. With reward functions aligned, the end-to-end baseline reaches only $18.3\%$ mean success across training budgets up to $2$~M steps and remains highly seed-sensitive, while the hierarchical design trains reliably in $200$~k steps. We therefore characterize the hierarchy as beneficial and easier to train, rather than strictly necessary.

Future work will focus on three directions: scaling to a larger seed campaign with finer success-rate granularity (50--100 evaluation episodes per seed), validating guidance-law enhancements that address the persistent \texttt{disadvantage} bottleneck, and expanding training diversity to improve generalization across aggressive offset-attack geometries.
