\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a hierarchical guidance architecture that combines a virtual pursuit point (VPP) generator with a line-of-sight rate (LOS-rate) guidance law, trained and tuned via a bilevel PPO and CEM-EMA framework. Three revised conclusions emerge from our study.

First, hierarchical decomposition is valuable because it embeds a physically correct guidance law inside the learning loop. By delegating low-level command generation to the LOS-rate guidance law, the learned policy can focus on high-level geometric decisions. On the JSBSim F-16 model, physically motivated corrections to the guidance law---altitude hold, roll-angle protection, and bank-angle load-factor compensation---eliminate the previous crash in the disadvantaged stern-conversion scenario. A four-stage curriculum then removes seed variance and yields $100\%$ success across $16$ random training seeds and all four geometries.

Second, the VPP layer is better understood as a learnable augmentation of classical guidance than as a universal replacement. VPP and No-VPP perform identically on three of four JSBSim scenarios, and a fixed $+500$~m lateral virtual-point offset rescues the No-VPP baseline to $100\%$ on the remaining mild-disadvantage geometry. This shows that the decisive factor is not the learned offset itself, but the guidance law's physical correctness, the CEM-EMA optimized gains, and the curriculum that exposes the policy to the full scenario distribution.

Third, the theoretical framework developed in this paper is not merely descriptive but predictive. We experimentally validated three of its key predictions: VPP+LOS degenerates to PN under fixed default parameters, potential-based reward shaping does not alter the optimal policy, and CEM-EMA outperforms CEM-GD on flat gain landscapes.

Future work will focus on scaling to a 141-seed reproducibility campaign for statistical power and expanding training diversity to improve generalization across crossing geometries. The residual No-VPP mild-disadvantage timeout is closed in principle by any lateral virtual-point offset, so the remaining question is how to make such geometric corrections adaptive across unseen engagement geometries.
