\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Hierarchical Decomposition is Necessary}

The empirical advantage of the VPP+LOS-rate architecture over end-to-end DRL is not merely a hyperparameter difference: both architectures use identical PPO hyperparameters, observation spaces, and training budgets. The improvement stems from structural inductive bias. By fixing the guidance law to LOS-rate---a well-understood differential-geometric controller---the policy need only learn \emph{where} to place the virtual point, not \emph{how} to generate roll-rate, normal-overload, and throttle commands. This reduces the effective search space from the full command manifold to the lower-dimensional offset space, enabling faster convergence and better generalization.

The JSBSim transfer clarifies the source of this advantage. The original LOS-rate law crashes on the \texttt{disadvantage} scenario because it does not compensate for the vertical lift loss in a steep bank. Adding altitude hold, roll-angle protection, and load-factor compensation eliminates the crash. Thus the hierarchy is not valuable simply because it splits the problem into two learned levels; it is valuable because it isolates the aircraft-physics reasoning in a guidance law that can be inspected, corrected, and gain-optimized independently of the neural policy.

The end-to-end baseline's lower success rate is consistent with the broader DRL literature: end-to-end policies in continuous control tasks typically require an order of magnitude more samples to match modular designs. In safety-critical guidance tasks, this sample-efficiency gap is a decisive argument for hierarchical decomposition.

\subsection{VPP as a Learnable Augmentation of PN}

The capture-region analysis reveals that, under fixed default parameters, VPP+LOS behaves almost identically to PN direct. At first glance this might suggest the VPP layer is unnecessary; however, it is consistent with the analytical intuition captured by Proposition~2. The VPP layer becomes valuable only when the high-level policy is trained to select context-dependent offsets: lead pursuit to avoid collision singularities in high-aspect engagements, lag pursuit to prevent overrun at high speed ratios, and terminal blending near the minimum stand-off distance.

The JSBSim results further refine this interpretation. VPP and No-VPP achieve the same success rate on three of four high-fidelity scenarios, and both require the same physical corrections to the guidance law to avoid crashing. Only the mild-disadvantage geometry differentiates them: No-VPP times out, while VPP closes. Diagnosis of the 150 timeout episodes reveals that No-VPP initially closes to a minimum range of only $310$~m but then overshoots; the final antenna-train angle ($21.6^{\circ}$) is inside the success cone, so the failure is purely geometric, not a loss of control or crash.

The decisive follow-up experiment was to replace the learned VPP output with a fixed lateral offset of $+500$~m on the No-VPP checkpoint. This single displacement rescues the mild-disadvantage success rate from $0\%$ to $100\%$ ($30$ episodes) and also succeeds on all other scenarios. It confirms that the learned VPP offset is functionally equivalent to a classical lateral-pursuit correction: it places the virtual point slightly beside the target, preventing the pursuer from overrunning the stern-conversion geometry. This single case is the legitimate domain of the learned offset, but it is not the dominant source of performance. We therefore reframe the contribution: the hierarchy's primary value is embedding a physically correct, CEM-optimized guidance law; the VPP layer is a learnable augmentation that approximates a simple, interpretable geometric correction in a subset of difficult geometries (Fig.~\ref{fig:no_vpp_diagnosis}).

\subsection{Implications of CEM-EMA and PBS}

The CEM optimizer comparison provides a clear engineering recommendation: EMA smoothing should be the default update mechanism for gain optimization. CEM-EMA is the only variant that converges to a perfect success rate on the regression scenarios, while CEM-GD degrades the quality of the search distribution despite achieving the same best score. This empirical pattern supports the theoretical recommendation to avoid gradient-ascent updates on flat or noisy gain landscapes.

The PBS ablation is consistent with the conceptual framework of Proposition~22: potential-based reward shaping should accelerate early learning without changing the final optimal policy. In our single-seed run, both conditions reached 50\% success at the same step and converged to the same final success rate. The Phase~2 A2' multi-seed ablation later found no statistically significant difference between dense-only and dense + PBS ($p = 0.785$), so PBS remains an optional theoretical enhancement rather than a necessary component.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations}

From a deployment perspective, the hierarchical architecture offers two practical advantages: (1) the guidance law can be certified or analyzed independently of the learned policy, and (2) the CEM-EMA gain optimizer can be re-tuned for different aircraft platforms without retraining the neural policy. These properties are particularly valuable in defense applications where interpretability and recertification are critical.
