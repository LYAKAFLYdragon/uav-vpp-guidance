\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Hierarchical Decomposition is Effective}

The empirical advantage of the VPP+LOS-rate architecture over end-to-end DRL is not merely a hyperparameter difference: both architectures use identical PPO hyperparameters, observation spaces, and---in the extended study---identical reward functions. The improvement stems from structural inductive bias. By fixing the guidance law to LOS-rate---a well-understood differential-geometric controller---the policy need only learn \emph{where} to place the virtual point (or whether to leave it at zero offset), not \emph{how} to generate roll-rate, normal-overload, and throttle commands. This reduces the effective search space from the full command manifold to the lower-dimensional offset space, enabling faster convergence and better generalization.

The end-to-end baseline's instability is consistent with the broader DRL literature: end-to-end policies in continuous control tasks typically require an order of magnitude more samples to match modular designs, and even then they remain sensitive to initialization. In safety-critical guidance tasks, this sample-efficiency and reliability gap is a decisive argument for hierarchical decomposition. We therefore characterize the hierarchy as \emph{beneficial and easier to train} rather than strictly necessary.

\subsection{Why the VPP Offset Does Not Help Here}

The 5-seed comparison shows that the VPP-enabled policy and the zero-offset policy achieve comparable success rates ($70.0\%$ vs $73.3\%$). Why does the learned offset not provide a clear advantage? Under the tested close-range, constant-velocity geometry, the LOS-rate guidance law already generates adequate lead-turn and collision-avoidance behavior when its gains are well tuned. The additional degrees of freedom introduced by the VPP offset therefore add learnable parameters without a commensurate geometric benefit. On failure episodes, the VPP policy occasionally selects large offsets that drive the aircraft out of bounds, which explains the significantly lower episode returns despite similar success rates. This does \emph{not} imply that VPP offsets are universally harmful; in regimes with stronger target maneuvers, larger stand-off ranges, or aerodynamic coupling, adaptive offsets could still be valuable. Within the scope of this study, however, they are non-critical.

\subsection{VPP as a Learnable Augmentation of PN}

The capture-region analysis reveals that, under fixed default parameters, VPP+LOS behaves almost identically to PN direct. This near-equivalence validates the baseline behavior of the guidance law and highlights that the learned component is not required for the core intercept behavior. The VPP layer becomes valuable only when the high-level policy is trained to select context-dependent offsets: lead pursuit to avoid collision singularities in high-aspect engagements, lag pursuit to prevent overrun at high speed ratios, and terminal blending near the minimum stand-off distance. In the present experiments, such contexts are not the dominant failure mode, so the learned offset does not improve success rate.

\subsection{Implications of CEM-EMA and PBS}

The CEM optimizer comparison provides a clear engineering recommendation: EMA smoothing should be the default update mechanism for gain optimization. CEM-EMA is the only variant that converges to a perfect success rate on the regression scenarios, while CEM-GD degrades the quality of the search distribution despite achieving the same best score. This empirical pattern supports the theoretical recommendation to avoid gradient-ascent updates on flat or noisy gain landscapes.

The PBS ablation is consistent with the conceptual framework of Proposition~22: potential-based reward shaping should accelerate early learning without changing the final optimal policy. In our single-seed run, both conditions reached $50\%$ success at the same step and converged to the same final success rate. The Phase~2 A2' multi-seed ablation later found no statistically significant difference between dense-only and dense + PBS ($p = 0.785$), so PBS remains an optional theoretical enhancement rather than a necessary component.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations}

From a deployment perspective, the hierarchical architecture offers two practical advantages: (1) the guidance law can be certified or analyzed independently of the learned policy, and (2) the CEM-EMA gain optimizer can be re-tuned for different aircraft platforms without retraining the neural policy. These properties are particularly valuable in defense applications where interpretability and recertification are critical. The zero-offset variant is especially attractive because it removes the VPP neural network entirely while retaining the guidance-law layer, simplifying deployment without sacrificing performance in the tested regime.

\subsection{JSBSim Curriculum and Fixed-Offset Validation}

The JSBSim results reconcile the apparent contradiction between early zero-offset failures on the mild-disadvantage geometry and the broader conclusion that the VPP offset is non-critical. On the full nonlinear F-16 model, both VPP and No-VPP policies with the standard three-stage reward curriculum reach $100\%$ success across $16$ random seeds on sinusoidal and weaving targets. The residual timeouts observed in a few seeds without curriculum disappear once angle-aware shaping and closing-rate normalization are added.

More importantly, a fixed $+500$~m lateral virtual-point offset rescues the No-VPP baseline to $100\%$ success on the mild-disadvantage geometry, while the zero-offset baseline times out. Diagnostic analysis shows that the timeout is not caused by loss of control or aerodynamic stall; the aircraft maintains $21.6^{\circ}$ antenna train angle and closes to within $5.9$~km, but the anchor-point chase produces overshoot under target speed advantage. The offset shifts the anchor laterally, giving the LOS-rate guidance law the geometric lead it needs to complete the offset intercept. This confirms that the learned VPP policy is effectively recovering a tunable anchor geometry, and that a simple fixed surrogate suffices for the tested geometries.
