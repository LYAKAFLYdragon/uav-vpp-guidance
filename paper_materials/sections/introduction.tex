\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Close-range UAV pursuit requires a controller that can close range, respect aircraft envelope limits, and maintain a favorable aspect angle while the target changes geometry. Classical proportional navigation (PN), augmented PN (APN), and differential-game guidance provide compact laws with clear assumptions~\cite{pn,zarchan2012,apn1971,shima2002}. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) offers a different route by learning a mapping from observations to control commands, and has been applied to simulated air combat and autonomous maneuver decision making~\cite{ppo,sb3,ernest2016,mn2019,hu2021bvr,li2022msddqn,fan2022a3c,yang2024drl,chai2023tsmc,wang2024kbs,zheng2024sci}. Each family leaves a practical question unresolved for this study: when a learned module is inserted above a classical guidance law, which part of the hierarchy is actually responsible for success or failure?

Virtual-pursuit-point (VPP) guidance is one such hybrid design. A high-level policy chooses an offset from the target, and a lower-level guidance law tracks the resulting virtual point. This structure resembles tactical pursuit-point decision making~\cite{mpn2022} and is consistent with hierarchical reinforcement-learning ideas such as temporal abstraction and options~\cite{barto2003hrl,kulkarni2016hdqn,bacon2017option,sutton1999options}. The engineering appeal is clear: the learned policy selects geometry, while the guidance law retains a physically interpretable command interface. The scientific issue is less settled. A VPP policy may help because it predicts target motion, because it shifts the target point into a safer geometry, or because the low-level guidance law and its gains are better tuned than an end-to-end baseline. Without a frozen comparison protocol, these explanations are difficult to separate.

\textbf{Literature gap.} Existing studies have examined tactical pursuit-point decision making, short-range air-combat DRL, and prediction-aware VPP guidance largely as separate design choices. Many comparisons vary the observation set, controller interface, training budget, simulator backend, or random seeds at the same time. This makes it hard to tell whether an observed gain comes from prediction, hierarchy, gain tuning, or an easier evaluation setup.

\textbf{Problem addressed.} This paper asks a narrower question: under a shared observation space, PPO training scaffold, random-seed protocol, and simulator backend, what evidence can be used to audit the VPP--guidance interface in close-range UAV tracking? The goal is not to claim a universally stronger controller. The goal is to locate the performance bottleneck under the current reward, interface, and scenario envelope.

\textbf{Contributions.} First, we define an auditable fair-comparison protocol for hierarchical VPP guidance, zero-offset No-VPP guidance, end-to-end DRL, and classical guidance baselines. Second, we report the currently available evidence from simple-backend and JSBSim F-16 evaluations, including confidence intervals, effect sizes, and seed-level caveats following deep-RL reporting recommendations~\cite{henderson2018deep,patterson2023empirical}. Third, we reframe the main empirical value as a qualified negative result: in the tested configurations, the performance gap is not explained by the predictor alone, and the VPP--guidance interface remains a plausible bottleneck requiring explicit ablation. Fourth, we provide executable scripts for the missing external-baseline, interface, and robustness experiments needed to freeze the final Defence Technology submission package.

\textbf{Boundary of claims.} All conclusions in this manuscript are limited to the scenarios, reward weights, guidance implementation, and available checkpoints in the repository. Stronger claims, including strict GRU superiority over LSTM, physical infeasibility of tail-chase geometries, or universal superiority of VPP over PN/APN and end-to-end DRL, are not made. Runs that do not yet satisfy the final 10-seed JSBSim protocol are labeled exploratory rather than used as conclusive evidence.
